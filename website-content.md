Building Business Thinking Early — With Structure, Depth, and Purpose
About Youngpreneurs Business School
Youngpreneurs Business School is an academic learning platform designed to introduce
students aged 8–11 and 12–18 to the fundamentals of entrepreneurship, economics,
marketing, and design thinking in a structured, age-appropriate, and intellectually meaningful
way.
We do not treat business education as a hobby or a short-term activity. At Youngpreneurs,
students follow a well-designed curriculum, similar in spirit to a business school — but
adapted carefully for young minds.
Our goal is not to create entrepreneurs overnight. Our goal is to develop thinking ability,
decision-making skills, and real-world understanding that benefits students in academics,
careers, and life.
Who Is This Program For?
Youngpreneurs is designed for:
● Curious students who ask “why” and “how”
● Learners who enjoy thinking, analysing, and applying ideas
● Parents who want more than activity-based classes
● Schools looking for meaningful entrepreneurship education
No prior business knowledge is required. The program builds concepts from the ground up.
Age Groups & Learning Levels
Ages 8–11: Foundation Level
At this stage, students are introduced to basic business and economic thinking through
stories, guided discussions, and simple real-life examples.
Students learn:
● What businesses do and why they exist
● Basic money concepts and choices
● Simple marketing ideas
● Introduction to problem-solving and design thinking
● Working in teams and expressing ideas
Learning is structured but playful, ensuring strong conceptual foundations without pressure.
Ages 12–18: Advanced Foundation Level
This level is designed for mature learners who can analyse, compare, and reason.
Students explore:
● Entrepreneurship and decision-making
● Market research and customer behaviour
● Marketing strategy and branding
● Economics and pricing fundamentals
● Design thinking and innovation processes
● Ethics, responsibility, and real-world impact
The focus shifts from what happens to why it happens.
Curriculum Structure
The Youngpreneurs curriculum is divided into 4 core modules, each consisting of 10 structured
chapters.
Module 1: Entrepreneurship
Students learn how ideas turn into ventures and how decisions are made.
Topics include:
● Entrepreneurship mindset
● Goal setting and planning
● Risk and reward
● Problem-solving frameworks
● Leadership and teamwork
● Ethics and responsibility
Module 2: Economics
Students understand how markets, money, and choices work.
Topics include:
● Demand and supply
● Pricing basics
● Market systems
● Competition
● Consumer behaviour
● Role of government and society
Module 3: Marketing
Students learn how businesses connect with customers.
Topics include:
● The 4Ps of marketing
● Branding and positioning
● Customer segments
● Digital marketing basics
● Communication strategies
● Real-world marketing analysis
Module 4: Design Thinking
Students learn how to solve problems creatively and systematically.
Topics include:
● Understanding user needs
● Idea generation
● Prototyping
● Testing and feedback
● Innovation mindset
● Turning ideas into solutions
Each module progresses from concept → application → reflection.
How Learning Happens
Youngpreneurs classes are designed to keep students mentally engaged, not passively
listening.
Each chapter includes:
● Clear conceptual explanations
● Real-life and age-appropriate case studies
● Role-play activities to practise decision-making
● Thinking-based classwork
● Home activities that apply learning to daily life
Students are encouraged to explain their thinking, not memorise answers.
Role Plays, Case Studies & Applied Learning
Learning is reinforced through:
● Role plays where students take on business roles
● Case studies based on realistic scenarios
● Decision-making exercises with multiple outcomes
● Application-based tasks instead of rote worksheets
These methods help students understand consequences, trade-offs, and reasoning.
Classwork & Home Activities
Our classwork and homework are designed to be:
● Thinking-oriented, not repetitive
● Open-ended where appropriate
● Connected to real-life situations
● Encouraging discussion at home
This ensures learning continues beyond the classroom without feeling like extra burden.
